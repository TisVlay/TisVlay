# Routable dials: the connect budget only spends against a network that exists

Status: **implemented and field-verified** (3685e01; baseline d4bf1f1, review round ed07bee, field check 2026-07-06). Kept active only until the PR #106 soak retires it.

## Problem

The resume carrier race, observed in field QA (2026-07-06): a dial's attempt budget (`CONNECT_MAX_ATTEMPTS` × `CONNECT_BACKOFF`) starts spending the instant its triggering event fires, while the NIC takes its own time coming back from suspend or boot. Both wakes that morning connected only by margin — the boot journal even shows two `WoL send failed, retrying` lines before the carrier arrived. A Wi-Fi desktop, slow DHCP, or STP port delays would spend the budget into a dead NIC, and the structural gap behind the race: **a spent wake dial is not `CarrierParked`** — parking happens only when a *live* connection loses its carrier/route — so the eventual carrier-up redials nothing and the TV stays off until manually asked. Every dial trigger has this race (boot replay, Startup/Resume wakes, rpc power-on fired before the network settles); resume is just where the trigger and the NIC cycle are causally synchronized.

## Design

The headline is mechanism *reduction*: instead of adding a re-arm-on-carrier-up compensation path, one rule — **no dial without a route** — makes the budget measure attempts against a network that can carry them, and the race dissolves. Scope: all network dials, plain and wake alike (decided 2026-07-06; one rule beats two).

**Scope honesty (from review): the gate covers route *absence*, not route *quality*.** `MonitoredInterface(Some(None))` means the kernel has no usable route to the TV's address — the NIC-down resume/boot window. A route that exists but doesn't carry (laptop roamed to a coffee-shop network: `ip route get` resolves via the default route) dials and burns its budget exactly as today, failing observably. That is the intended boundary: route absence is a fact the netlink monitor already owns; reachability is what the attempts are *for*.

- **Defer while routeless.** `reconcile_connections` skips a TV whose `MonitoredInterface` is `Some(None)`. The `PendingConnect` stays armed, wake flag intact, and **entering deferral resets `attempts` to 0** — deferral always resumes with a full budget, keeping the accounting coherent when an attempt was spent in the pre-first-resolution window before `Some(None)` arrived. When the monitor delivers a route, `RouteResolved` arrives as an inbox event and reconcile re-runs in that same batch — **the resume redial is structural (reconcile re-runs), not a new arm in `apply_route_resolutions`**; adding one there would double-dial. WoL then sends on a NIC that exists (the in-dial WoL retry loop is untouched — the guarantee is about when a dial *spawns*, not about bounding retries inside one).
- **The absence distinction is load-bearing.** `MonitoredInterface` exists only on network TVs after their first resolution — and route monitoring is requested *as the first dial spawns* (per-dial, from the reactor's `SpawnConnect`/`Facts` arms), not at enrollment. So: component absent (CEC, mock fixtures, any first dial) → dial as today; `Some(None)` → defer; `Some(Some(_))` → dial. The guard must be exactly `matches!(monitored, Some(MonitoredInterface(None)))` — treating *absent* as defer would deadlock every existing fixture and stall first dials. This is also what keeps pairing safe: a fresh pairing's first dial always runs with the component absent (no resolution can precede the dial that requests it), so `PairVerifying`'s fail-on-first-dial terminal fires promptly as today.
- **Routeless failures return the attempt but keep the backoff.** In the `ConnectFailed` arm, when the route is absent at failure time: don't increment `attempts`, but still schedule the normal `RetryAt` backoff before clearing `Reconnecting`. The backoff is what bounds a flapping route — an oscillating uplink settles to at most one dial per backoff period with the budget unspent, instead of a spawn-per-flap loop. The invariant, stated where the code lives: **attempts count only route-carried dials; backoff is wall-clock courtesy either way.** Ordering inside the arm: the `PairVerifying` pairing terminal stays first (a pairing dial that fails must fail its pairing, never defer), then the route-absent defer branch, and only when a route exists does the existing budget comparison run — so a routeless failure can never publish the budget-spent `failed` phase.
- **Observability instead of a timeout.** Entering deferral inserts an `AwaitingRoute` marker component (house one-concept-per-component style; removed when a dial spawns or the `PendingConnect` goes away) — insertion is the single-shot publish edge for an `awaiting_network` phase on `progress/power`, wake dials only (they are the only phase publishers today; the name matches the pairing vocabulary's `awaiting_*` and the gerund tense of `connecting`). The sequence reads as a prefix — `awaiting_network → wol_sent → connecting → connected` — and may legitimately recur within one wake if the route drops mid-dial; phase consumers must tolerate the reversion. The marker is a publish gate only; the dial gate is the `MonitoredInterface` check, so the two facts can't drift. Deferral is unbounded: a TV that's asked to connect keeps wanting to until the network can carry the attempt — the dual of "a TV that goes down stays down until asked."
- **Deferred consumers inherit the wait, deliberately.** A surviving `DispatchVerify` (screen escalation/elevation riding a wake dial) holds harmlessly across the deferral — verification already defers while a dial is pending — and re-asserts on the eventual route-carried connect; its `dispatch-failed` terminal is deliberately traded for the unbounded wait here, with `awaiting_network` as the observable. Manual `reconnect` rpcs and config-patch redials defer silently by the same rule (plain dials are phase-silent; they ack early today and still do).

What this deliberately does not change: a TV unreachable while the network is fine still burns its attempts and fails observably. `CarrierParked` keeps its one meaning (a live connection lost its carrier). CEC never carries `MonitoredInterface` and is structurally untouched.

## Implementation sketch

Steps 1 and 2 are one atomic change — the reconcile guard is what makes the no-increment `ConnectFailed` state safe (without it, a cleared-`Reconnecting` routeless `PendingConnect` would busy-dial every batch).

1. World, `reconcile_connections`: query gains `Option<&MonitoredInterface>`; extract the full dial gate into one named predicate ("no live handle, no in-flight dial, backoff elapsed, and a route present or route-monitoring absent") so the conditions live in one place. On deferral: reset `attempts`, insert `AwaitingRoute` (publishing `awaiting_network` for wake dials on insertion); remove the marker when a dial spawns.
2. World, `ConnectFailed` arm: `StateWrites` widens with a `MonitoredInterface` read query (it has no access today). Arm order: `PairVerifying` terminal → route-absent branch (no increment, keep `RetryAt` backoff, clear `Reconnecting`) → existing budget logic. State the attempts-count-route-carried-dials invariant in a comment at this arm and in architecture.md.
3. Hygiene while there: route the inline `PendingConnect` constructions (escalation arm, wake-dial pre-flight) through `redial()` so the component has one constructor.
4. Tests, in `tests/carrier.rs` (the route-lifecycle home; `RouteResolved` injection is already its fixture idiom): (a) raw `AddTv` + `RouteResolved(None)` + a Resume-shaped wake → zero dials and one `awaiting_network` phase (`add_connected_tv` can't be reused — it blocks on connect); (b) `RouteResolved(Some)` then fires the wake with a full budget (connect count 0 → 1); (c) mid-dial route loss doesn't consume an attempt but does back off; (d) the existing route-present budget contract — extend an existing budget-exhaustion pin with a route-present precondition rather than duplicating it if one covers it.
5. Docs: extend architecture.md's connection-lifecycle sentence in place (the "single dial gate" wording is exactly what the route gate refines; `AwaitingRoute` joins the marker enumeration); docs/watchers/network.md notes route resolution now gates dial spawning in addition to parking.

## Field verification (2026-07-06)

The adversarial variant of the motivating race, on the packaged stack: desktop to sleep, ethernet cable pulled, resume, cable back roughly a minute later. Observed in the journal: the route retraction landed ~110 ms *after* the Resume dispatch, so one wake dial spawned against the stale pre-sleep route (the documented boundary — the gate governs when dials spawn, not what's already in flight), ran its in-dial WoL loop against the dead NIC, and failed routeless; the attempt was returned and reconcile deferred with **zero dials for the ~35 s the cable stayed out** — the window where the old behavior had already spent the budget and gone quiet forever. Cable back → route restored → `LG TV online after wake` four seconds later. One wart fixed in the same round: the in-flight dial's best-effort WoL resend logged per-iteration at warn (62 lines in 30 s); demoted to debug (the resend is best-effort by contract and the dial's real outcome surfaces through `ConnectFailed`), while the bounded `wake_with_retry` attempts keep their warn as the useful diagnostic.

## Non-goals

- No budget inflation or backoff tuning — the race is removed, not slowed.
- No overall deferral timeout — unbounded by design, observable via `awaiting_network`.
- No change to `CarrierParked` semantics or to CEC dial behavior.
- No gating on route *quality* — a present-but-non-carrying route burns budget and fails loudly, as today.
- No re-arm-on-carrier-up compensation path — deferral makes it unnecessary.
- No cancellation of an in-flight dial on mid-flight route loss — it fails on its own schedule, its attempt is returned, and deferral owns everything after.
