# A wake reports success only once the TV has acted on it

## Goal

A power-on that the TV doesn't act on should keep trying for a short while, then fail visibly, instead of reporting success with the TV dark. Today two backend paths call a wake done before the TV has acted on it, and the world reports whatever they say:

- **LG's `wake_and_connect`** returns as soon as SSAP registers. An LG in `ActiveStandby` (for a few seconds after a remote-off) still accepts SSAP, so the dial connects on its first attempt, sends one WoL, and returns. On the #228 hardware checks, a power-on 1 s after a remote-off connected on attempt 1 and the TV stayed in `activestandby` until the remote turned it on; `progress/power` said `connected`.
- **CEC's in-place `power_on`** (a power-on over the live link of a TV in standby, the path most CEC power-ons take, because a CEC TV stays connected in standby and CEC emulates screen actions through power) sends one Image View On and returns. A frame the TV ignores leaves it off, and nothing reports it.

This retires [concerns/power-on-unverified.md](../concerns/power-on-unverified.md).

## Background

- **The contract.** [docs/backends/implementing-a-vendor.md](../backends/implementing-a-vendor.md): "`wake_and_connect()` owns *all* retry/backoff/probe logic for the wake path — the daemon calls it and treats it as atomic." Link and power are separate axes (`state/link`, `state/power`); `progress/power` is the wake flow's progress.
- **History.** `645f94de` (the dispatch QoS tiers) left power-on out of the verify tier on purpose, since "`power_on` already has WoL + retry inside": its reliability was delegated to each backend's wake. `378439a8` gave CEC's dial-path wake a re-send-and-poll loop after a field failure; the in-place `power_on` never got one.
- **The world's routing is already right** after #228: in place for a standby-reachable bus, a wake dial for a network TV that needs waking, satisfied for an awake one. What's missing is anyone checking that the wake landed.
- **What the reviews found** (design, implementation correctness, maintainability), folded in below: failing LG's dial on a refused wake would make the world treat a working link as a failed connect (publishing `connect-failed`, re-running the wake dial up to `CONNECT_MAX_ATTEMPTS`, then dropping the link); LG's power query can take ~25 s through `with_retry`'s hidden reconnect, so an attempt count doesn't bound wall time; CEC's `reachable()` accepts `ToOn`, which `is_awake` rejects; a TV that answers power queries with `Unknown` would fail every wake; re-claiming CEC's logical address in place would drop RC passthrough; and the trait docs, not the guide, are the contract's canonical home.

## Decisions

1. **Backends do the transport-specific trying; the world judges the outcome once.** A backend's wake (dial or in place) resends and polls within a short wall-clock confirm window, then returns what it has: the connection for a dial, `Ok` in place. It errors only for link problems, never because the TV refused. The world then judges the wake from the power state it leaves. Reason: the retry primitives (WoL, Image View On, re-claim) are transport-specific and stay in the backends, as the guide says; one vendor-agnostic verdict in the world covers every backend, the mock (the guide's template) and any future one, without a second retry layer, and keeps a working link up. Rejected: a world verify tier that retries (a second layer over the backends' loops); failing the dial on a refused wake (above).
2. **A wake failed only if the TV positively reports an off state:** `PowerState::class() == PowerClass::Off` (`Standby`, `ActiveStandby`, `Suspend`). `On`-class states (`Active`, `ScreenSaver`, `ScreenOff`) and `Transient` ones (`WarmingUp`, `CoolingDown`, `Unknown`) don't fail it: a warming-up TV has acted on the wake, and a TV that can't report its power state gets no false failure. The backends' loops poll until an `On`-class report or their deadline. No new core API.
3. **On `progress/power`, a wake dial ends `connected` unless its connection's power snapshot is `Off`-class, and then it ends `failed` with the link kept up.** No key, payload, phase or decode rule changes, so these are `fix` commits; the `PowerPhase` rustdoc says what each terminal phase now means.
4. **An in-place power-on publishes only `failed`,** when the state read after the command is `Off`-class. A success publishes nothing, so the tray's wake toasts don't fire on every CEC screen-on. It goes through an internal `BackendEvent::PowerOnFailed`, handled in `facts.rs` next to the dial-failure arm, so it shares that arm's cleanup (dropping a riding screen-on intent, which would otherwise escalate into a second in-place wake). No new wire value.
5. **An off-going command interrupts an in-place wake.** While an in-place `PowerOn` runs, the connection task also takes commands; a `PowerOff` or `ScreenOff` abandons the wake and runs (latest intent wins, as a later off already cancels a held wake), so an `on_sleep` power-off can't miss the PC's sleep behind a wake. Other commands wait.
6. **Failures use `Error::Backend`,** as CEC's dial path does today. A new `tisvlay_core::Error` variant would be published API for a case the world doesn't need to tell apart.
7. **The contract lives in the trait docs** (`TvConnector::wake_and_connect`, `TvConnection::power_on` in `tisvlay-core`'s `traits.rs`), stated once; the vendor guide gives a clause and a link, and `architecture.md` describes only the world's verdict.
8. **No new tuning places.** LG's wake budget becomes one wall-clock deadline (replacing `WAKE_CONNECT_MAX_ATTEMPTS` as the bound) plus a short confirm window; CEC's `WAKE_VERIFY_*` constants are renamed `WAKE_CONFIRM_*` so they don't read as the world's verify tier (`RetryConfig`, `DispatchVerify`), which stays screen-only.
9. **Loops are tested under paused time with seams,** not against sockets: each backend's loop is factored over closures (connect, read power, send wake), so LG's needs no fake SSAP server and no real WoL broadcast; `tisvlay-lg` gains `tokio`'s `test-util` dev-feature. CEC's fakes gain `FakeCecLink::with_ignored_wakes(n)`.

## Steps

Each step carries its own doc and comment updates, so the tree's docs match its code after every commit.

1. **World: judge a wake dial by its connection's power snapshot.** In the `Connected` arm for a wake dial (`facts.rs`), publish `connected` unless `state.power_state` is `Off`-class, else `failed`; the link stays. Docs: `PowerPhase` rustdoc (`tisvlay-protocol`), `BackendEvent::PowerPhase` docs, `architecture.md`'s key table and power-path paragraph. Tests (world, on the mock): a wake dial that connects into `ActiveStandby` (`set_initial_power_state_override`) publishes `failed`, keeps `state/link` connected, and publishes no `connect-failed`; into `Active`, `connected`; into `WarmingUp` or `Unknown`, `connected`. Check `escalation_reasserts_on_reconnect_then_gives_up`, which wakes into `ActiveStandby`, and adjust what it asserts about `progress/power` if needed.
2. **LG: the wake resends WoL until the TV is on, within a deadline.** Factor `wake_and_connect`'s loop over closures. After a connect, poll the raw client's power state (not `LgConnection::get_power_state`, whose `with_retry` reconnects underneath); treat a timed-out or unreadable poll as "not on yet". Resend WoL (`wol::wake_best_effort`) each interval; return the connection at an `On`-class report or when the confirm window ends; the connect loop runs to a wall-clock deadline. Tests under paused time: already `Active` returns at once with no extra wake; `ActiveStandby` for N polls then `Active` returns after N+1 polls with wakes sent between; never on returns the connection at the window's end; timed-out polls don't end the loop. Docs: the LG connector's constant and comment docs (including "the only retry layer"), `TvConnector::wake_and_connect`'s rustdoc (the contract), the guide's clause, and `lg/firmware-quirks.md`'s remote-off latch section.
3. **CEC: the in-place power-on resends and polls.** Put the send/sleep/poll skeleton beside the `CecLink` seam (`link.rs` or a new `wake.rs`), used by the connector's `wake_and_connect` and by `CecConnection::power_on`, with a closure for a send error: the dial path re-claims as now, while in place resends on the held link only (no re-claim, so RC passthrough survives). Poll early and back off, resending every `WAKE_CONFIRM_DELAY`; `Err(ConnectionClosed)` still returns at once (it drives `PowerOnLost`). `power_on` returns `Ok` after an `On`-class report or the window's end. Tests on `FakeCecLink` under paused time: `with_ignored_wakes(2)` wakes in place on the third send; `with_ignored_wakes(usize::MAX)` returns after the window with the TV in standby; `power_on_wakes_in_place_on_the_existing_link` moves to paused time; the dial-path tests and the daemon's `cec_power_cycle_keeps_the_link_up` keep passing. Docs: `TvConnection::power_on`'s rustdoc (replacing its stale fallback sentence), the CEC connector and connection module docs, `docs/cec/protocol.md` (its in-place description and fallback), `docs/cec/testing.md`.
4. **World: an in-place power-on that didn't land is reported, and an off interrupts it.** After an in-place `PowerOn`, the task's existing state refresh reads the TV; if it's `Off`-class, the task sends `BackendEvent::PowerOnFailed`, which `facts.rs` turns into `progress/power` `failed` with the dial-failure arm's cleanup. While the `PowerOn` runs, the task also takes `PowerOff` and `ScreenOff`, which abandon it. The GUI's power-on progress follows a standby-reachable TV for up to the in-place window. The mock gains `set_power_on_ignored` (named after `set_power_on_closes`): an in-place power-on that leaves the TV in standby. Tests: world (`failed` published, link up, screen-on intent dropped; a `PowerOff` sent during a slow in-place wake runs at once), GUI (a late `failed` reaches the row). Docs: the book's troubleshooting phases, `lg/benchmarks/wake-reconnect.md`'s CEC power-cycle note.
5. **Hardware check** (unattended, logging to a file, CEC Image View On as the safety net, as in #228's probe). Before each iteration, restore the precondition (TV awake, LG link up, both getters reading on), and log the cached power state when the power-on is sent. Record `tisvlay watch` with timestamps for `progress/power` and `event/connect-failed`.
   - LG: a CEC `<Standby>`, then an LG `power on` after 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 s, twice each: each ends with the TV on and `connected`, or `failed` with the link still up. A send at 0.2 s may find the cache still `Active` (LG reports the transition ~200 ms later), which the world treats as already on; log it as its own outcome rather than a failure of this change.
   - CEC in place: `power on` to the CEC TV from standby, several times; the TV wakes each time.
   - Optional, with the maintainer at the remote: a real remote-off, then `power on`.
6. **Retire.** Delete the concern and its index entry, move the plan's lasting decisions into their homes (the trait docs, `architecture.md`), delete this plan and its Active plans entry, and sweep inbound references.

## Open questions

- **The confirm windows' lengths.** LG's must outlast the remote-off latch (`lg/firmware-quirks.md`: about 1 s, with turn-on accepted after ~5 s) without holding a refused wake long; CEC's in place must be short enough that commands queued behind it (only non-off ones, after decision 5) don't stall. Proposed: a few seconds each, settled by step 5.
- **A power-off during a dialing LG wake isn't cancelled** (`cancel_held_wake` leaves a wake that's already dialing), so the confirm loop can resend WoL briefly after an off. The short window bounds it; a mid-loop awake-to-off edge could also stop it.
- **A screen intent's in-place wake** never reaches `dispatch-failed` (`verify_dispatch` sends `PowerOn` and drops the latch). No current backend is standby-reachable without emulating screen through power, so this is noted, not fixed.
